# Alphas - The Beginning
## Chapter 6: Betrayal of Blood

Frank and Amanda sprinted across the red-grey silt of the loading bay, their eyes scanning the rusted cranes and eaves for any sign of the cameras Idris had warned about. They reached the heavy steel door just as the first sixty seconds bled away.

"Amanda, look," Frank whispered, his flashlight beam cutting through the darkness to reveal a rack of ruggedized servers tucked into a reinforced corner of the warehouse. "They're storing the data locally."

The servers sat hummed with a faint, almost imperceptible vibration. There were no thick power cables snaking across the floor, and no connection to the overhead industrial lines.

"I wonder how they're charging them," Frank thought aloud, stepping closer to inspect the base of the unit.

He noticed a series of thick, insulated leads running downward, disappearing beneath the heavy concrete floor tiles. "They aren't using the grid at all," Frank realized. "They must have a localized power source buried sub-surface, maybe a high-capacity chemical battery or a silent hydrogen cell. It's a closed loop. No heat signature on the roof, no noise for the neighbors, and no paper trail on a utility bill."

"Idris, do you see this?" Amanda hissed into her comms, held close to her mouth. She knelt by the humming rack, pulled a rugged data-link cable from her bag, and jammed it into the server's primary maintenance port. "I'm physically tethered. I'm launching a mobile hotspot to bridge the air-gap now."

Back at the mahogany desk, Idris's eyes widened as the handshake signal turned green. "Connection established! That's why the building looked dead, it's an air-gapped system running on an isolated DC circuit. It's completely invisible from the outside unless you're plugged in exactly where you are.

"Can you pull the data?" Frank asked, his eyes darting back toward the door. "We have less than two minutes left on the clock."

"The bridge is stable, but the encryption is fighting the transfer," Idris replied, his fingers a blur across the keys. "Amanda, don't let that cable nudge or the tunnel collapses."

As the link stabilized, Idris's monitors back at the house began to populate with encrypted directories. This wasn't just a standard log, it was a localized repository, a digital vault for the TIA's deepest secrets.

"I'm in," Idris whispered, his eyes scanning the flickering file names. "The data's all encrypted, so I'm flying blind, but the file sizes are massive."

"Download everything!" Frank urged, his flashlight beam trembling slightly as he swept the surrounding darkness.

"I'm pulling it through your phone's uplink, but it's crawling," Idris replied, his brow furrowed in deep concentration. He began dragging the folders into his encrypted drive, the progress bar mocking him with its slow pace.

Suddenly, a low, mechanical thrum began to vibrate through the warehouse floorboards. It wasn't the servers; it was the guttural idle of a heavy engine just outside the loading dock, pulsing through the hollow structure like a predatory heartbeat.

"Frank, someone's here," Amanda hissed, her eyes widening as she prepared to rip the cable out, sharp daggers of light cutting through the cracks in the corrugated steel.

Outside, the rest of the group sat paralyzed. The sudden glare of high-beams reflecting off the warehouse walls turned their car into a target. The arriving vehicle didn't just pull up; it dominated the space, its heavy tires crunching over the red-grey silt with a slow, menacing intent.

"It's them," Callie whispered, her shadow dancing erratically against the upholstery. "William, they're going to block the exit!"

William didn't answer. He was staring at the vehicle's insignia, a sharp, silver corporate crest. While it looked familiar, his mind was racing too fast to place it; all he knew was that it represented the very threat they had been running from. The sheer presence of the vehicle made the air in the car feel impossible to breathe.

Inside, Frank didn't hesitate. "Amanda, yank it! We have to go!" he barked into the comms. She disconnected the cable in one swift motion, killing the link. He didn't wait for the progress bar to finish. Lunging for Amanda's arm, he pulled her behind a stack of rusted pallets just as the heavy sliding door at the far end of the warehouse screeched open, flooding the pitch-black space with blinding, artificial light.

They didn't stay to see who stepped out of the SUV. Taking advantage of the long shadows and the booming distraction of the door, they slipped through the side exit. They sprinted across the red-grey silt, their hearts hammering against their ribs, and scrambled into the idling car where the others waited in a state of high-alert panic.

Back at the house, Idris watched the stolen data packets finalize on his screen. The download was incomplete, the connection severed the moment Amanda pulled the tether, and all they could do now was pray that the fragments they held were enough to break the machine.

"So, did you manage to decrypt those files?" Frank asked as they arrived back. The entire drive back, Idris had been silent on the radio as he had been focused on decrypting. Frank was still vibrating from the adrenaline of the escape, pacing the room with a restless energy, needing to know if their brush with death in Sector 7 had been for nothing.

"Well, no," Idris said with a somber nod, his eyes fixed on a progress bar that hadn't moved in twenty minutes. "It is heavily encrypted. This isn't just a standard password; it's a nested, multi-key protocol. It's designed to be unbreakable without a physical hardware token."

He turned the monitor so the group could see the lines of nonsensical, shifting code. "I have a feeling these might be much more useful than the data I already have. Up until now, I've only had their basic financial ledgers. They show that their expenses and income don't tally—that massive sums of money just disappear. It's evidence of greed, but it's dry. It doesn't tell us the story."

Idris leaned closer to the screen, the blue light reflecting in his exhausted eyes. "But if they are willing to encrypt these files with a 256-bit rotating cipher, it means we've found the heart of the machine. This isn't just accounting; it's the 'how' and the 'who.' The problem is, my software can't brute-force this. It would take a century."

"So we're stuck?" Peter asked, his voice sinking.

"Not stuck," Idris muttered, "just slow."

"We can't just sit here and watch a progress bar," Amanda said, looking at the group. "If we all stay cooped up in this room, Lumivox is going to know something is wrong. We need to look normal. We need to head back to the university."

"She's right," William added, though his eyes were still glued to the encrypted files. "We have classes, labs, and lives. If we disappear, we're basically handing them a confession that we're the ones who breached the warehouse. We go back, keep our heads down, and act like stressed-out students until Idris breaks through."

"I'll stay," Idris said firmly. "I'm already a ghost. I'll keep the rig running and alert the group chat the second the shell cracks."

The next twenty-four hours were a grueling test of endurance. While the rest of the group walked the campus halls, jumping at every vibration of their phones, Idris remained alone in the mahogany chair. He had built a custom "cracking rig" by daisy-chaining the group's left-behind laptops to increase his processing power.

Finally, exactly one day after their escape, a sharp, rhythmic chirp erupted from the monitors.

As the decryption finalized, Idris slumped back in the mahogany chair, the rhythmic chirp of the monitors sounding less like a victory and more like an alarm. For twenty-four hours, he had been a ghost in a machine of his own making, fueled by caffeine and the desperate need to outrun the silence of the room. Every time the progress bar stalled, he saw Zach's face in the reflection of the glass, not the smiling genius from the Baltis labs, but the terrified boy who had begged him to stop before the fire took everything. The guilt was a physical weight, a "twelve-pound lie" of his own that he carried every time he risked this new group of friends. He stared at his trembling hands, realizing that by daisy-chaining their laptops to build this rig, he had once again tethered the people he cared about to a digital grenade.

The "Project Grey-Suit" files began to unfold, but for a moment, the text blurred. He felt the same suffocating pressure that had drowned him in Baltis, a localized flashback to the orange glow of the highway fire triggered by the flickering blue LEDs of his servers. He wanted to reach for his phone, to call his parents just to hear a voice that didn't sound like a tactical report, but he let his hand drop. He had to be cold; he had to be the vault. With a jagged breath, he forced his emotions back into the shadows and typed the coded message to the group: The Lion is caged. Get here now.

Tthe "Project Grey-Suit" files was unfolded into a sprawling network of illicit transactions. Idris stood back, allowing the group to see the high-resolution scan of a confidential distribution ledger. The list was a "who's who" of regional power, beginning with the high-ranking officials in Baltis who had turned a blind eye to the illegal dumping at the TIA.

But as Idris scrolled further down, the air in the room grew cold. The list shifted from city titles to local municipalities. "It's not just the city," Callie whispered, her finger tracing a line on the screen. "Look at the planning board, the water commission... these are Riverview names."

The realization hit them like a physical blow: the corruption wasn't a contained infection in the city; it was a local epidemic. Then, the scrolling stopped at the very bottom of the Riverview section. Under a high-level category for Zoning and Environmental Waivers, a single name sat in bold, clear font: Marcus Vance.

The room went deathly silent. Marcus Vance was William's uncle, the man who had mentored him and stood as the town's pillar of legal integrity. But the files revealed a much darker truth. Marcus hadn't just taken bribes; he was the primary signatory for a secret expansion project. He had signed off on a plan to turn a massive stretch of Riverview wetlands into a secondary dumping ground, a mirror of the Sector 7 TIA. The project was scheduled to begin in less than a month.

"He didn't just know about the corruption," Peter said, his voice hard. "He's the one who made it legal here. He signed the warrant for this town's death."

William stepped back from the monitor, the betrayal feeling like a lead weight in his chest. Every memory of his uncle's lectures on the "rule of law" now felt like a calculated performance.

"He's been working for them the whole time," William whispered, his shock sharpening into a cold, trembling fury. "While he was telling me to fight for the little guy, he was the one selling the little guy out. He's the reason this is happening to our backyard."
