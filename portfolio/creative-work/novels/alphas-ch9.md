# Alphas - The Beginning
## Chapter 9: Kernel Panic

"Well, would you look at that," Zach said, his eyes tracking the cluster of red dots speeding along the highway on his tablet screen. He turned the device around so Amanda could see the map. The signals were moving fast, Northbound. Away from the warehouse. Away from her. "It seems your friends have found a sudden urge to visit the coast," Zach said with a mock sympathetic pout. "They aren't coming for you, Amanda. They're cutting their losses." He walked closer to the chair where she was tied, inspecting her like a specimen. "Don't look so surprised. You'll get used to it with Idris. He talks a big game about loyalty, but when it comes down to the wire, Idris doesn't care about people. He cares about righteousness." "Why would you betray him?" Amanda asked, her voice steady despite the fear tightening her chest. "He mourned you. He spent years trying to find out what happened to you." "You still don't know the guy, do you?" Zach laughed, shaking his head in disbelief. "Idris doesn't do friends, Amanda. He does crusades." Zach leaned against a stack of crates, crossing his arms, his voice dripping with disdain. "Yeah, we were 'best friends' on paper. But I barely saw the guy. I was a ghost to him long before the accident. He was an absolutely boring dude. No parties. No concerts. No girls. He was always too busy worrying about his soul to worry about his friends." Zach sneered, gesturing vaguely at the ceiling. "He's got two loves in his life: Justice, and God. And let me tell you, there isn't any room left in that heart for actual human beings. The only time he actually made time for me was when I brought him the corruption data. I wasn't his friend, Amanda. I was just a chance for him to play the holy crusader. And clearly... he's decided that saving you isn't part of the divine plan." He paused, a flicker of genuine frustration crossing his face. "I actually cared about the guy, you know. I tried to save him. When Lumivox first pulled me in, I warned him. I told him to back off. But he wouldn't listen. He started rambling about 'innocent people suffering,' as if that was his personal burden." Zach looked at her, his eyes narrowing with envy. "That's the joke of it all. Idris absolutely hit the jackpot in life. Amazing parents who adore him, a decent living, some decent talent. He has everything. And at the end of the day... he wastes it all on unnecessary stuff. He uses a golden ticket to fight battles that don't even matter." "For me on the other hand, everyday life is a survival and with my talent when I get this opportunity, I certainly am not leeting it go at the cost of anybody, why would I let my 'best friend' who doesn't act like it destroy it' Zach turned away, looking at his own reflection in the dark screen of a monitor. "For me, on the other hand? Life has always been a survival game. I didn't have his safety net. So when my talent finally bought me a way out, when I finally got this opportunity you can be damn sure I wasn't going to let it slip." He looked back at her, his expression hardening. "I am certainly not going to let it go just to satisfy his moral compass. Why would I let my 'best friend', a guy who never acted like one to begin withburn my future to the ground?"

Idris had commandeered the wheel, and Frank was rapidly coming to the conclusion that this was a terrible idea. The car was screaming for its dear life, bouncing violently over the uneven road. In the rearview mirror, Frank could see the others launching toward the ceiling with every pothole, looking like they were involuntarily auditioning for the high jump. "Hey! Ease off!" Frank cautioned, clutching the dashboard as the tires fought for traction. "We aren't going to be good help to Amanda if we're dead." Idris didn't speak a word. He didn't even tap the brakes until the warehouse loomed out of the darkness. He skidded the car to a violent halt, kicking up a cloud of dust, and was out the door and sprinting toward the entrance before the others had even unbuckled their seatbelts.

"Idris! Wait!" Frank shouted, fumbling with his seatbelt, but the heavy metal door of the warehouse slammed shut before he could even open the car door. Inside, Idris didn't slow down. He didn't check corners or look for cameras. He ran on pure, blind fury, his footsteps echoing like gunshots against the concrete floor as he sprinted toward the light at the end of the hall. He burst into the main floor, his chest heaving, his eyes wild and then he stopped. The air seemed to vanish from the room. The fury that had been fueling his heart was instantly replaced by a cold, hollow vacuum. Amanda, slumped in the chair, jerked her head up. A wave of staggering relief washed over her at the sight of him, he had actually come. But as she watched him, that relief curdled into a sharp, cold worry. She saw his knees buckle. She saw the color drain from his face until he looked like a ghost himself. He wasn't looking at her with the triumphant gaze of a rescuer; he was looking at Zach with the hollow eyes of a man watching his entire world collapse. The face was older and the suit was expensive, but it was him. This was the friend Idris had wept for, the man whose name he had carried like a lead weight of guilt for years. Idris had been accused of Zach's murder by the public, but he had accused himself of it every single day since. "Zach...?" The name was a broken whisper of pure, unadulterated pain. Idris ignored the guards and the weapons pointed at his chest. He only saw the phantom standing there in the flesh, breathing and smiling. "You're alive," Idris wheezed, his face contorting. "All this time... I thought I killed you." "You're late, Idris," Zach said, his voice smooth and devoid of the warmth Idris remembered. He didn't offer an embrace; instead, he checked his watch with a casual, practiced flick of his wrist. "Technically, you did kill me. The Zach you knew, the one who sat in server rooms dreaming of changing the world, he died in that wreckage. Lumivox just gave the survivor a much better offer." Amanda watched them, her heart hammering against her ribs. She wanted to scream for Idris to run, to realize that this wasn't a reunion, it was a trap. The way Zach looked at him wasn't with friendship; it was with a predatory, calculated hunger. "I spent years looking for your grave, Zach," Idris said, taking a staggering step forward. "I let them call me a murderer because I felt like one. I almost gave up on everything because I couldn't live with the fact that I survived and you didn't." Zach let out a sharp, mocking bark of laughter, pacing a slow circle around the bound Amanda. She flinched as he passed, his presence feeling like a cold shadow. "And that's your problem, isn't it? You're so wrapped up in your own 'moral burden' that you never stopped to think about mine," Zach sneered. "You were the golden boy, Idris. The one with the perfect life and the righteous soul. I was just the shadow. The guy who did the actual work while you prayed for the results." Zach stopped and leaned down, whispering loud enough for Idris to hear. "I didn't need your guilt. I needed your talent. And since you wouldn't give it to me willingly back then... I'll take it now." "You used me," Idris whispered, the pain in his voice hardening into something sharper. "The 'System Admin'... you weren't just hunting a fugitive. You were hunting me." "I was testing you," Zach corrected with a chillingly polite smile. "And honestly? Sending your phone on a bus was the first interesting thing you've done in years. But look at you now." Amanda's eyes darted from the guards to Idris. He was standing in the open, defenseless, paralyzed by a ghost. She had never seen him look so small, and it terrified her more than the ropes cutting into her wrists.

"Well, Mr. Idris," Zach said, stepping aside to reveal a sleek, humming terminal he had pulled from the shadows. "Can you please enter the password to this deadman's switch?" He tapped the screen, and a complex, jagged encryption prompt flared to life, bathing the room in a cold blue light. "I might have stolen the file from your local drive during the raid, but for some reason, I can't crack the container," Zach admitted, his tone conversational, as if they were back in their old university lab. "It seems you've been doing some impressive work on your encryption protocols lately. Truly top-tier." Zach's smile sharpened, turning predatory as he glanced at the guards and then back at the bruised, bound Amanda. "But your physical security? Your firewall?" Zach let out a short, dismissive snort. "Not so much. You were so busy guarding the data that you forgot to guard yourself. In fact, I have to thank you for giving me the keys to the kingdom. Your GitHub repositories were a roadmap, Idris. All I had to do was study your architecture and forge a sword that could pierce through." He leaned in closer, his voice dripping with triumphant malice. "And baiting you into accessing the server? That was the easiest part. I knew your conscience wouldn't let you stay away once you saw the breadcrumbs I left about Sector 7. You're so predictable, Idris. Your 'righteousness' isn't a shield, it's a handle I can use to pull you wherever I want." He gestured toward the keyboard, his eyes gleaming. "The switch, Idris. Unlock it. Prove to me that all that talent of yours is actually worth something."

"Wait... you knew?" Idris asked, his voice cracking with disbelief. "You knew I broke into the Sector 7 server and you just... sat there and did nothing?" He took a step toward Zach, his mind racing through the documents they had risked everything to steal. "What about the data we obtained? The evidence of the human trials? The environmental reports? People are going to see that, Zach! The truth is out there because you were too arrogant to stop us." Zach let out a slow, chilling chuckle, shaking his head as if Idris were a naive child. "Oh, Idris. You still think this is a game of 'gotcha,' don't you?" Zach straightened his suit jacket, his expression completely indifferent. "With a machine as large as Lumivox, some people and some data, are just collateral. That's the first lesson I learned from them. You think those reports will bring them down? They'll be buried in litigation for a decade while the company pivots to something else." He stepped closer to Idris, his eyes cold and empty. "I let you take that data because it was a small price to pay to find you. I sacrificed a few files and a handful of low-level executives just to flush my old friend out of the tall grass. It was a trade, Idris. And looking at you now, I'd say I got the better end of the deal."

Idris stared at the blinking cursor. His mind, usually a whirlwind of logic, was struggling to focus through the haze of betrayal. But as his fingers hovered over the mechanical keys, he noticed the command line syntax. Zach, you ego-maniac, Idris thought. You're still using the 'Aegis' shell we built in junior year. "What's the matter, Idris?" Zach mocked. "Forget the password to your own soul?" "I'm just making sure," Idris whispered, his eyes scanning the bottom corner of the screen. He saw a tiny flickering pixel, a legacy bug from their old code that Zach had never been able to fix. It meant the Administrative interrupt was still active. Idris didn't type a password. He typed a sequence of hexadecimal characters that looked like gibberish to a spectator but acted as a "logic bomb" for the specific version of the Aegis shell Zach was running. If I can trigger a kernel panic, Idris reasoned, the hardware is hard-wired to dump a status report to the local emergency frequency. "Hurry up," Zach snapped, stepping closer. "Fine," Idris said, his voice cold. "You want the truth? Here's the only truth that matters." He slammed his finger onto the Enter key. For a heartbeat, nothing happened. Then, the blue light of the terminal turned a violent, strobing red. A high-pitched, piercing shriek erupted from the warehouse overhead speakers, the internal fire and security alarm. "What did you do?" Zach yelled, shielding his eyes as the terminal screen began scrolling white text at light-speed. "I didn't unlock the data, Zach," Idris shouted over the siren. "I told the warehouse the reactor core is melting down. In thirty seconds, every automated door in this building is going to seal, and the local fire department is going to get a high-priority distress signal." The guards panicked, looking at their flickering tablets. The "invincibility" Zach had projected was shattered by a simple line of legacy code.

The siren's shriek was deafening, a physical force that rattled the metal rafters of the warehouse. Red emergency lights began to strobe, painting the room in a rhythmic, bloody hue. "You idiot!" Zach roared, his face twisting with rage. "You've tripped the lockdown protocol!" Panic surged through the room. The guards, trained for combat but not for a high-level system failure, fumbled with their tablets as the screens flashed "CRITICAL SYSTEM FAILURE." Zach didn't stay to argue; he turned and bolted toward the server room at the back of the floor, desperate to override the command from the master hub. At that exact moment, the heavy side-door of the warehouse groaned as Frank finally forced it open. He charged into the main floor just as the automated security system reached its fail-safe point. "Idris! Amanda!" Frank yelled, his eyes locking onto the chaos. Zach, blinded by his own haste, collided with Frank near the entrance to the server annex. They tumbled into the small, reinforced room just as the pneumatic locks engaged. A heavy, reinforced steel shutter slammed down with the force of a guillotine, sealing both Zach and Frank inside the soundproof chamber. "Frank!" Idris screamed, but he was cut off as a heavy boot slammed into his ribs. Despite the alarms, the guards hadn't fled. Their orders were clear: the assets were not to leave. While the automated system shrieked above them, two guards tackled Idris, pinning his arms behind his back with jagged plastic zip-ties. They dragged him across the concrete, shoving him down onto the floor right next to Amanda's chair. "Don't move!" one guard barked, his rifle leveled at Idris's head. The warehouse was now a tomb. Zach and Frank were trapped together in the server room, and Idris lay bound on the floor, inches away from Amanda. "Idris," Amanda whispered, her voice trembling as she looked at him through the strobing red light. "Idris, look at me." Idris groaned, his cheek pressed against the cold concrete. He looked up, his eyes meeting hers. For the first time, they were alone in the middle of the storm, both of them bound, both of them at the mercy of a ghost who was currently locked in a room with their only hope of rescue.

Frank scrambled to his feet, his breath coming in ragged gasps as he wiped dust from his forehead. He looked up, and the words died in his throat. Even in the dim, pulsing emergency lighting of the sealed room, the face was unmistakable. "You..." Frank whispered, his eyes widening. "You look familiar. I've seen you in the old photos. In Idris's files." Zach stood up and brushed off his charcoal suit, perfectly composed despite having just been tackled. He adjusted his silk tie and looked at Frank with a chilling, predatory grin. "And so do you," Zach replied, his voice echoing off the reinforced steel walls. "Mr. Frank Davis. Engineer extraordinaire. Current lead candidate for that prestigious PhD scholarship, isn't that right? I am truly amazed, man." Frank felt a chill that had nothing to do with the warehouse's air conditioning. "How do you know about the scholarship? The board hasn't even made the final announcement." "I've read your proposal, Frank. Truly inspired work on sustainable grid architecture," Zach continued, ignoring the question as he paced the small, locked space like a caged wolf. "A mind like yours is on the cusp of everything. You have the talent to change the world, yet here you are, wasting your potential in a suburban basement playing bodyguard to a fugitive." Zach stepped into Frank's personal space, his grin never wavering. "You're a candidate for greatness, Frank. But Lumivox? We are the board. We decide who gets the funding and who gets forgotten. Tell me, is 'doing the right thing' worth watching your name get crossed off that list?" "What do you mean?" Frank asked, his brow furrowing in a deep frown. "You built your life from scratch, didn't you, Frank?" Zach asked, his voice dropping to a smooth, dangerous silk. "You worked twice as hard as guys like Idris just to get a foot in the door. Do you really want to see it all collapse? Or worse..." Zach leaned in, his eyes locking onto Frank's, draining the air from the small room. "Do you want to see your family and friends constantly looking over their shoulders? Do you want them looking at their backs for the rest of their lives just because you found this little conspiracy so 'interesting'? You're a brilliant mind, Frank, probably the only one here who is actually my intellectual equal. But a hero? A hero's family is just a target. Is Idris's crusade worth their peace of mind?" Frank flinched, his jaw tightening as the blood drained from his face. Zach watched the flicker of pain and felt a surge of professional satisfaction. He didn't move to attack; he simply let the weight of the threat hang in the air. Seeing that reaction, Zach's grin widened. He knew he had hit a sore point, the one thing Frank valued even more than his career: the safety of the people he had spent his life protecting. Zach had stopped talking about data and started talking about blood, and he could see Frank's resolve beginning to fray under the sheer terror of what Lumivox could do to the people he loved. Frank stood frozen, the red emergency lights painting his face in rhythmic flashes of warning. He looked at Zach, then at the heavy steel door that separated them from the chaos outside. In the silence of the sealed room, Zach's words felt like a slow-acting poison, making Frank picture his family, people who had no idea they were now being weighed as "collateral" on a corporate balance sheet. "You're a monster," Frank whispered, his voice thick with a mixture of fear and pure, concentrated loathing. "I'm a realist, Frank. There's a difference," Zach said, leaning back against a server rack. "Monsters kill for fun. I'm offering you a life. I'm offering you the ability to walk out of here, take that scholarship, and move your family to a neighborhood where they don't have to lock their doors at night. All you have to do is stop being an obstacle." Zach tilted his head, watching Frank's hands. They were still balled into fists, but they were shaking. "Think about it," Zach urged, his voice dropping to a supportive, brotherly tone. "If you stay on this path, what happens? You think you're helping Idris? Look at his life, Frank. Look at what his 'righteousness' cost the people he actually loves." Zach stepped closer, his eyes glinting with a cruel sort of pity. "Do you think Idris's parents are living the dream? Ever since he started this crusade, they've lost their freedom. They're looking over their shoulders every time they go to the grocery store. They had to go into hiding, Frank. They're prisoners in their own lives because their son decided he wanted to be a martyr for the truth." Frank flinched, the image of Idris's parents, kind people who had lost everything flashing through his mind. "Is that what you want for your parents?" Zach hissed. "To have them spend their golden years in a safe house because you wanted to play hero? To have your sister change her name and move three states away just to stay off a Lumivox watchlist? Idris can live with that guilt because he thinks God will reward him for it. But you? You're not like him. You actually care about the people, not the principle." Zach looked toward the door, then back at Frank, his eyes cold and calculating. "The doors are going to open eventually. When they do, you have a choice. You can try to be the hero and tackle me again, or you can just let me go. Step aside. Let me walk out of that door and disappear. You can tell Idris the mechanism jammed. I don't care." Zach leaned in, his voice a lethal whisper. "Let me go, Frank. Save your career. Save your family from the 'prison' Idris put his own parents in. Let him keep his 'right thing' while you keep the people you love safe. Isn't that what a real provider does?" The silence in the room was heavy, broken only by the muffled, distant wail of the sirens. Frank looked at Zach, the man Idris called a brother, now a snake offering him his family's safety in exchange for his soul. Frank didn't say a word. He didn't move away, and he certainly didn't step aside. Instead, he reached out and grabbed Zach by the lapels of his expensive suit, his grip so tight his knuckles turned a ghostly white. But the defiance in his eyes was clouded. Zach's words had landed like lead weights in his stomach. The image of Idris's parents, living as fugitives, stripped of the quiet, happy life they deserved played on a loop in Frank's mind. He looked at Zach, and for a moment, he didn't just see a villain; he saw the harbinger of a future he was terrified to provide for his own parents. "I'm not letting you go," Frank rasped, his voice trembling with a cocktail of rage and burgeoning grief. Zach didn't struggle. He stood perfectly still within Frank's grasp, a small, knowing smirk playing on his lips. He could feel the tremor in Frank's hands. He knew the poison was working. "You can hold onto me all you want, Frank," Zach whispered, looking down at the shaking hands clutching his jacket. "But you're not holding me. You're holding onto a ghost of a life you've already lost. Every second we stay in this room, the 'interest' in your family grows. Every siren you hear outside is another nail in the coffin of that scholarship." Frank's breathing was shallow, his eyes darting toward the steel door. He was trapped in a nightmare of logic. If he let Zach go, he betrayed Idris and everything they had fought for. If he held on, he was essentially signing a warrant for his family's misery. He didn't let go, but his head slumped forward until his forehead pressed against the cold, metallic fabric of Zach's chest. He was holding onto his enemy not as a captor, but as a man drowning who didn't know which way was up. "Look at you," Zach said, his voice almost tender in its cruelty. "You're mourning them already. You're a good son, Frank. A brilliant engineer. And yet, here you are... choosing a friend who wouldn't even consider the same sacrifice for you." The hiss of the hydraulic locks began to echo through the room. The system was resetting. The door was about to open, and Frank Davis had only seconds to decide if he was going to be the man who saved the mission, or the man who saved his mother.

While Frank was paralyzed by Zach's poison in the server room, the heavy atmosphere on the warehouse floor was shattered. Peter and William didn't have the luxury of firearms, but they had the advantage of surprise and the raw momentum of desperate men. They had left Callie and Belinda at the perimeter, keeping them in the safety of the shadows while they moved in to strike. The two guards holding Idris and Amanda were still distracted by the flickering pings on their tablets and the strobing red lights. They never saw the rescue coming. Peter moved like a shadow, closing the distance in a low sprint. He didn't shout; he just acted. He launched himself at the guard holding the rifle over Idris, using his body weight to drive the man into the concrete. Simultaneously, William came in from the flank, catching the second guard in a brutal clinch before he could level his sidearm, disarming him with a practiced twist of the wrist. "Idris! Amanda! Move!" Peter grunted, pinning his opponent to the floor in a frantic struggle. William managed to kick the fallen weapons away, his chest heaving. "Get them out of those ties! Now!" Idris scrambled to his feet, his hands still bound behind him, but his eyes were fixed on the server room. The hydraulic hiss of the locks reached a crescendo. The heavy steel door finally slid upward, revealing the two figures inside.

The hydraulic hiss of the door reached its peak, and the steel shutter slid upward to release the two men from their metallic tomb. Peter and William had successfully taken the drop on the guards. Peter was pinning one to the floor, while William stood over the other, his chest heaving as he held a confiscated rifle. They both looked up, their eyes darting to the opening door, weapons ready, but they stopped, confused. They didn't recognize the man in the expensive suit. To them, he was just another high-level Lumivox executive. But they saw the way Frank was holding him. "Frank! Who is this?" Peter shouted, his eyes darting between the prisoner and his friend. "Is this the one in charge?" Zach didn't flinch. Even with his back against the server rack, he looked over Frank's shoulder with a smug, knowing smirk. "Tell them, Frank," Zach whispered, his voice still a lethal silk. "Tell them why you're going to let me walk out of here. Tell them about what happens to your family if I don't check in tonight." Frank's head stayed bowed for a heartbeat longer. The mental image of his mother in a Lumivox "safe house" was a knife in his gut. But then, he took a breath, sharp, jagged, and cold. He looked up, and the defeat Zach had been preying on was gone, replaced by a grim, terrifying clarity. "You're right about one thing, Zach," Frank said, his voice low and vibrating with fury. "My family is everything. And I'm not going to let them live in a world where people like you are free to touch them." Before Zach could react, Frank's grip tightened. He didn't push him away; he slammed him back against the server rack, the metal groaning under the impact. "You think you can bait me with a scholarship?" Frank hissed, his face inches from Zach's. "You think I'm going to let you walk so I can take a check from the people who want to put my friends in the ground? You don't know me at all. You're not an 'equal.' You're just a coward hiding behind a legal team." Zach's smirk finally vanished. For the first time, a flicker of genuine alarm crossed his face. He tried to shove Frank back, but Frank was an anchor, unyielding and heavy. "Peter! William!" Frank roared, never taking his eyes off the man he was pinning. "This is him. This is the 'System Admin.' This is the ghost Idris has been chasing!" Peter and William stood paralyzed, their eyes locked on the man in the expensive suit. "Frank, what are you saying?" Peter asked, his voice low and dangerous. "Who is this guy?" Frank didn't loosen his grip for a second. He kept Zach pinned against the vibrating server rack, his shadow cast long and jagged by the strobing red lights. He turned his head just enough to look at his team, his voice thick with a mixture of betrayal and iron-clad resolve. "This is Zach," Frank said, the name sounding like a curse. "Idris's former best friend. The one who was supposed to be dead. The one Idris has been mourning for years." William took a step forward, his knuckles white on his confiscated weapon. " The one who made Idris a fugitive?" "He didn't die, William," Frank hissed, slamming Zach back against the metal once more as the man tried to squirm. "He just traded his soul for a Lumivox paycheck. He's been the one hunting us. He's been the 'System Admin' watching us from the shadows, using everything he knew about Idris to tear his life apart." Zach let out a dry, rattling laugh, even as he struggled for air. "I didn't tear it apart, Frank. I just showed him what it was really made of. And I offered you the same clarity. Too bad you're too small-minded to take it." Frank ignored him, looking straight at Idris. "He tried to get in my head, Idris. He told me what they did to your parents. He tried to tell me my family was next." Idris, finally standing tall after Peter had sliced the last of his ties, walked toward the server room. The hollow vacuum that had been in his chest moments ago was filling with a cold, focused clarity. He looked at Zach, not as a ghost, not as a brother, but as the architect of his misery. "He's not a friend anymore," Idris said, his voice steady for the first time that night. "He's just the man who thinks he can buy our silence with fear." Peter and William closed the circle, their confusion replaced by a grim, protective anger. They didn't need to know the history to recognize a traitor. "The alarm is still going, and more of them will be coming," William warned, glancing at the warehouse entrance. "What's the play, Idris? Do we take him with us or leave him for the fire department?"

The sirens outside shifted from a distant wail to a deafening, localized roar. Blue and red lights flooded the warehouse windows, cutting through the thick dust and strobe-red security lights. At the perimeter, Callie and Belinda didn't stay hidden. They saw the fire trucks and first responder vehicles screaming toward the loading docks and immediately went into action. Callie grabbed a high-visibility flashlight from the back of their car, waving the first responders toward the side entrance Peter and William had breached. "In here! There are people trapped near the server annex!" Callie shouted. By bringing the public authorities in, she ensured Lumivox couldn't "disappear" anyone quietly. Inside, the heavy bay doors were forced open by fire crews in full gear. The sudden influx of fresh air and bright white light shattered the claustrophobic tension. Frank didn't let go of Zach until the first two firefighters were less than ten feet away. He gave Zach one final, forceful shove against the server rack before stepping back, his hands raised but his eyes still burning with a protective fire. "He's the one you want," Frank said, gesturing toward Zach. "He's been holding these people hostage." Zach, ever the chameleon, immediately smoothed his suit. "I am a senior executive with Lumivox!" he barked at the responding police sergeant, his voice regaining its oily calm. "I have immunity under the Sector 7 security act. Call my legal counsel immediately." The sergeant didn't budge. He looked at the digital manifest on his tablet, then back at Zach. "I've got a problem with that, sir. You don't have an ID, and according to every federal database we have, the man you're claiming to be died three years ago." Zach froze. The blood drained from his face as the trap he had built for himself—his own "death", became his prison. "You were very thorough, Zach," Idris said, stepping into the light. "You wiped your records. You stayed off the grid. You let the world believe you were a ghost so you could hunt me from the shadows. But now? You're just a Jane Doe in an expensive suit." "Idris, tell them!" Zach hissed, his composure finally cracking as the handcuffs clicked shut. "Tell them I was not in that car!" Idris looked at him with a quiet, devastating pity. "The Zach I knew died in that wreckage. I have no idea who you are." As the officers led the screaming, "non-existent" man away, Callie and Belinda burst through the doors, running toward the group. Callie threw her arms around Amanda, while Belinda checked over Idris and Frank. The warehouse was no longer a tomb; it was a crime scene where the ghost had finally been caught in his own shroud.
