# The Cross: A Mirror and a Comfort
### Does the cross convict us and comfort us?

On Good Friday, our eyes are drawn to the cross. It is the place where our Savior gave His life, demonstrating an unfathomable love for us. It reveals the depths to which He was willing to humble Himself, enduring the whip, the mockery, and the shame. As we reflect on His sacrifice, the cross stands as two powerful things in our lives: a mirror and a comfort.
First, the cross is a mirror that reveals our broken state. Consider Peter in Luke 22. Here was a man who boldly declared just the night before, "Lord, I am willing to die with you." He was the one who proclaimed Jesus as the Messiah. He drew a sword and cut off a servant's ear to defend Him. He had left everything behind to listen to His words, and Jesus even called him the rock on which He would build His church.
Yet, when the shadow of the cross loomed and Jesus was arrested, even this devoted disciple surrendered to the weakness of his flesh. When the rooster crowed and the captive Lord turned to look at him, Peter saw the reality of his own failure. He went outside and wept bitterly. We see profound repentance here.
The crucifixion teaches us that despite everything we do in our lives, our achievements, our status, our qualifications, or even our deep knowledge of the Bible, none of it ultimately matters in earning our salvation. We are all human, constantly failing and falling short of God’s grace. Many times, we try to define our salvation by claiming we do not commit "big sins" like others do, and we become quick to judge. But when we do this, the cross neither convicts us nor comforts us, because we are trying to gain comfort from our own actions. It is just like the parable of the Pharisee and the Tax Collector. The Pharisee prayed out loud, proudly thanking God he was not a sinner like the other man, while the tax collector stood at the back and pleaded, "Lord, have mercy on me, a sinner." No one can claim to be without wickedness, because we were all the reason Jesus had to be crucified.
This brings us to a profound choice. Look at the two criminals hanging beside Jesus. The sight of the cross convicted one, leading him to repentance, while the other remained unbothered and chose to mock. When we look at the cross today, how do we respond? Does the sight of it convict us, or does it leave us feeling nothing? If we look at the cross and continually choose to stay in our sin, we are, like the unrepentant thief, mocking the very death of Christ.
Jesus had to go through death on the cross and not any other way because it was a death of ultimate atonement. It was the necessary sacrifice to atone for the sins of us all. On that cross, Jesus took upon Himself the punishment we deserved as sinners. He endured the eternal damnation that was meant for us. The agonizing weight of our sins was so immense that it caused Christ to cry out in the darkness, “Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?” ("My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?").
Secondly, the cross offers us profound comfort. It is the very place where Jesus secured our salvation, giving us the privilege of calling God "Abba, Father." It reassures us that even when we wander far from Him, the way back is always open. Every drop of blood He shed, every lashing He received, was poured out in love for each and every one of us.
Imagine if He had looked down and said, "I have had enough. These people are never going to repent." What would our state have been? Instead, He knew the intimate details of all our lives and despite it all, He went through with the crucifixion. That is where we find our hope. We have a God who loves us so much that He is willing to die for us, so why should we worry?
Yes, knowingly and unknowingly, we still sin and fail to deny our flesh. But when we repent, we have the comfort of knowing we are never too far gone. We can return to a Savior who will lovingly embrace us, even if the world frowns upon us and is ready to throw stones. This is the ultimate comfort of the cross: it gives us the opportunity to have a deeply personal connection with God, to speak to Him daily like a friend through all our trials. Just as it gave Peter the grace to get back up and become the leader of the flock, the cross gives us the grace to rise again.
As we get closer to camp, let us start thinking about this: what does the cross really mean to us? Does it actively affect our lives, or is Good Friday just another day we go to church and come back? As we focus on discipleship at this upcoming camp, let us ask ourselves if we are truly grateful for Christ's sacrifice. Let us allow the cross to be the mirror that shows us our need for a Savior, and the comfort that gives us the grace to rise up and follow Him.
