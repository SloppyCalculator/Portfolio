# Alphas - The Beginning
## Chapter 10: The Extraction

The atmosphere in Mrs. Potts' living room was thick with a mixture of exhaustion and heavy, lingering dread. The yellow glow of the lamps did little to warm the cold reality of what they had just survived; the air felt thin, as if the house itself were holding its breath.

The last few days had been a blur of high-stakes pressure and psychological warfare. In a final, desperate play at the warehouse, Zach had almost succeeded in rewriting history, using his polished charisma to convince the arriving authorities that Idris was the true villain. For a few agonizing hours, the truth hung in a precarious balance while Idris stared at the cold walls of an interrogation room. Unfortunately for Zach, he had underestimated the quiet brilliance of the man he once called a brother. Idris had successfully retrieved the hidden CCTV footage from the central server, a digital witness that stripped away Zach's corporate mask in high definition. Zach now sat in a cell, officially charged with fraud, kidnapping, and possession of illegal firearms. Alongside him, Dr. Thorne had been hauled in for faking Zach's death. At William's firm insistence, the group had also surrendered the documents associated with Marcus to the police. It was a victory for the village, the authorities concluded the contracts were signed under the pretext of blackmail, bringing Lumivox's predatory expansion to a grinding halt but the victory felt hollow. Despite the evidence, all three arrested men refused to cooperate. It wasn't loyalty keeping them silent; it was a visible, paralyzing terror. Even behind bars, they were clearly more afraid of someone higher up in the Lumivox shadows than they were of the law. Peter looked over at William and felt a surge of genuine alarm. The man who was usually the group's jovial, steady anchor had completely vanished. William sat with his head in his hands, his broad shoulders trembling under the weight of a devastating realization. To the world, Marcus was a corporate criminal, but to William, he was the uncle he had loved and trusted his entire life. The betrayal wasn't just corporate; it was familial. Every childhood memory now felt tainted by the fact that the man who had raised him was willing to sell the village's soul for a Lumivox paycheck. William looked as if he had aged a decade, his eyes hollowed out by the grief of being the one to hand his own blood over to the law. But it was Idris whose face truly betrayed the depth of the trauma. He sat motionless, the silence of the room echoing the void in his chest. The one person he had loved like a brother since childhood had spent years orchestrating his ruin. To Zach, Idris was just collateral. The betrayal was a double-edged blade: the friend he thought he had was a monster, and his only other true friend, Jordan, was truly gone. Idris was finally a free man, but he was standing in the wreckage of every relationship he had ever known. "So... do you think Lumivox would assist these guys to fight their cases?" Peter asked, his voice low as he finally broke the suffocating silence. He looked around the room, seeking some kind of strategic hope, but found only shadows. "I don't think so," Idris replied, letting out a short, hollow laugh that lacked any real mirth. He stared at the floor, his voice dropping to a cold, clinical tone. "To Lumivox, they aren't partners; they're just liabilities now. The big guys won't send lawyers, they'll send cleaners. They'll do everything in their power to cut the ties that link the board of directors to those three. Zach, Thorne, and Marcus are yesterday's news. They've already been erased from the corporate ledger." The group let that sink in. The men who had destroyed their lives were now being abandoned by the very monster they had served, left to rot in the dark just as they had once left Idris. "Well, Jordan's body wasn't recovered either," Belinda added, her voice a fragile whisper. A small, desperate flicker of hope ignited in her eyes as she gripped her tea mug until her knuckles turned white. She searched the room for even a single nod of agreement, needing someone to validate the possibility. Amanda noticed the visible tremor in Belinda's hands and leaned in, offering her new friend a supportive squeeze. "So, how does it feel to not be a fugitive anymore?" Amanda asked Idris, her voice softening as she gave his shoulders a comforting rub. "Well... it feels okay," Idris replied with a slight, tired smile. It was the first time in years he didn't feel the phantom weight of a target on his back, yet the silence of the "ghosts" still sat heavy and suffocating on his chest. In the corner, Callie remained standing, her eyes tracing the room until they settled on Frank. While the others were processing the news of the arrests and the hope for the missing, Frank had been a statue of silence. He sat rigid, staring at his own hands as if he could still feel the phantom pressure of Zach's lapels against his palms. The psychological poison Zach had poured into his ear, the threats against his family and the calculated destruction of his future seemed to be screaming louder than the conversation in the room, drowning out the very victory they were supposed to be celebrating. Suddenly, without a word, Frank stood up and walked toward the door. The floorboards creaked under his heavy, hurried steps as he vanished outside into the night air. Idris raised an eyebrow, looking at the others with concern. Callie gave a small, silent signal, a quick nod, letting them know she would handle it. She slipped out after him, leaving the rest of the group to sit in the quiet aftermath of their victory.

Callie stepped out onto the front porch, the cold night air hitting her face like a sharp reminder of the world beyond the warehouse. She found Frank leaning against the wooden railing, but he wasn't just standing there, he was collapsing into himself. His hands weren't just shaking; they were vibrating with a violent, uncontrollable tremor. His breaths were coming in shallow, jagged gasps, a terrifying, rhythmic wheezing that sounded like he was drowning on dry land. Even in the dim light, she could see the wet tracks of tears rolling down his face, his skin pale and slick with a cold sweat. "Is it back?" she asked, her voice dropping to a sharp, concerned whisper. She was the only one in the group who knew. She was the only one who had been there the first time his brilliance had been nearly crushed by the weight of his own anxiety, the only one outside of his family who had ever seen the "Engineer Extraordinaire" reduced to a terrified boy in a dark room. To Idris and the others, Frank was the rock. To Callie, he was a human being with a breaking point. Frank didn't, couldn't look up. He was doubled over, one hand clutching the railing so hard the wood groaned, the other pressed against his chest as if trying to manual-start his own heart. "I can't... I can't get it out," he wheezed, the words barely escaping his throat through the constriction of his airway. "His voice... it's stuck. He knew everything, Callie. The scholarship... my mom... he knew the street, the house number..." He let out a choked, sob-like gasp, his knees buckling until he was almost kneeling on the porch floor. The panic attack was a physical predator, convinced that even though the warehouse was miles away, the threat was still closing in. "He made it sound like... like I was the one hurting them," Frank managed to stutter, his eyes wide and unfocused, darting around the dark yard as if expecting a Lumivox strike team to emerge from the trees. "He said Idris's parents lost their freedom because of him... and that I was doing the same thing. I'm the reason they're in danger. It's me. I signed their death warrants the moment I didn't let him go." Callie moved quickly, kneeling in front of him to break his line of sight to the dark yard. She grabbed his icy, shaking hands, pinning them between hers to force him to feel the solid reality of her presence. "Frank, look at me! Focus on my voice," she commanded, her tone a steady, unwavering anchor. "That is the panic talking. That is the poison Zach injected into you because he couldn't find a firewall to hack. You are not the threat, Frank. You are the only reason they're still safe. Zach didn't win, he's in a cell because you didn't flinch." Frank's head fell onto her shoulder, his chest heaving as he fought a losing battle for oxygen. He was the group's protector, the man who built their defenses, but right now, in the silence of the porch, he was just a son terrified that his own excellence had cost his family their lives. Frank's words were broken, punctuated by sharp, desperate gasps for air as he clung to Callie's jacket. His tears were hot against her shoulder, a stark contrast to the freezing night air. "What is the purpose... of... these.. stuff?" Frank managed to wheeze out, his body trembling so violently that the porch swing nearby rattled. "In the end of the day, the good guys struggle for the rest of their lives. Idris struggled... had to be a fugitive... all because he wanted to do the right thing. Why does this even happen?" He pulled back just enough to look at her, his eyes bloodshot and wide with a haunting realization. "Zach is right. Being a hero is a luxury for people who have nothing to lose. I have everything to lose, Callie. My dad, My mom, my sister... they don't even know their lives are being gambled with. Why is the 'right thing' always the thing that hurts the people we love?" Callie held his gaze, her hands never leaving his. She could feel his pulse racing, a hummingbird caught in a cage of ribs. She knew this was the core of his trauma: the terrifying math of sacrifice. "Because the 'wrong thing' is easier, Frank," Callie said, her voice dropping to a low, fierce whisper. "Zach chose the easy path. He chose to be a ghost so he never had to feel the weight of a consequence. But look at him now. He's in a cage made of his own lies. He has no one. No family to protect, no friends to walk out on a porch for him." She reached up, wiping a stray tear from his cheek with her thumb. "The struggle isn't a sign that you're failing, Frank. It's a sign that you're still human. Idris didn't struggle because he was a fugitive; he struggled because he refused to let them turn him into a Zach. And you did the same thing in that room." Frank's breathing began to slow, the jagged edges of his panic smoothing out as he focused on the pressure of Callie's hands. "You didn't gamble with their lives," she continued firmly. "You saved them. If you had let Zach go, he would have owned you forever. You would have been his puppet, and they would have been his leverage for the rest of your life. By standing your ground, you took his power away. You ended the game." Frank let out a long, shaky exhale, leaning his forehead against hers. The silence of the night started to feel less like a threat and more like a reprieve. He wasn't fully okay, the fear for his family was a shadow that wouldn't disappear overnight but the crushing weight on his chest had finally begun to lift. Frank pulled himself together with a final, shuddering breath. He wiped his face, though his eyes remained raw and bloodshot. He couldn't go back inside, not yet. He couldn't look at Idris's relieved face or hear the group's hopeful planning while the sound of Zach's voice was still echoing in the back of his skull like a death sentence. "I can't go back in there, Callie," Frank whispered, his voice steady but fragile. He started to back away toward the steps of the porch, his car keys already gripped in his shaking hand. "I need to... I need to see them. I need to make sure the door is locked." "Frank, wait—" Callie started, reaching out. "Just tell them I'm exhausted," he interrupted, his pace quickening as he hit the gravel driveway. "Tell them the adrenaline crashed and I went home to sleep. Make something up. Please." Before she could argue, he was in his car. The engine turned over with a low growl, and Callie watched as the red glow of his taillights vanished down the dark suburban road. He wasn't going home to sleep; he was going to sit in his mother's driveway and watch the street until the sun came up. Callie stood on the porch for a long moment, the cold wind whipping her hair across her face. She looked at the front door, knowing the group was inside celebrating a victory that Frank was currently paying for in terror.

When she finally stepped back into the warmth of Mrs. Potts' living room, the conversation died down. Idris looked up from the table, his brow furrowed as he noticed she was alone."Where's Frank?" Idris asked, looking toward the door. "Is he okay?" Callie forced a small, practiced smile, though her heart was still heavy with the memory of Frank's breakdown. "He's hit a wall, Idris. The adrenaline finally let go. He asked me to tell you he's headed home to crash said he'd be back on the grid first thing in the morning." Idris nodded, though a flicker of doubt crossed his face. "I should have checked on him. He did the heavy lifting in that server room." "He just needs sleep," Callie lied, her voice firm. "We all do." The front door groaned as Mrs. Potts finally entered the room. Her eyes were clouded with an exhaustion that went deeper than bone, and her movements were heavy and sluggish, as if the weight of the day were a physical burden. She had spent the last several hours navigating a bureaucratic nightmare. Not only was she managing the logistics of the arrests, but her phone had been vibrating incessantly with calls from the villagers. Overcome with a sudden, crushing guilt for how they had treated her and Idris, they were now pleading for forgiveness, a chorus of apologies that she simply didn't have the energy to process. While Marcus remained at their local precinct for interrogation, Mrs. Potts had made the grueling trip over to Riverview herself. She wasn't taking any chances; she had stayed to ensure that Zach and Dr. Thorne weren't granted an easy exit or a quiet bail through some corporate loophole. However, seeing their faces had offered no satisfaction. Their absolute refusal to cooperate, their eyes fixed on the walls in a state of catatonic terror didn't make her life any easier. She had secured the bars around them, but the wall of silence they maintained was a chilling reminder that the true masters of Lumivox were still watching from the dark. She looked at the gathered team, her gaze lingering on their weary faces, and let out a long, ragged sigh that seemed to deflate her entire frame. Belinda immediately rushed over, wrapping her arms around her mother in a tight, protective hug. "Is everything sorted?" she asked, her voice hushed with hope. "Not exactly, kiddo," Mrs. Potts replied, returning the embrace with a weary, wry smile. She leaned back against the doorframe, her voice raspy from hours of negotiation. "We've got nothing but static from those three regarding the board of directors. They're too terrified to even breathe the name Lumivox in an interrogation room. But," she added, her eyes sharpening with a flicker of triumph, "the evidence of a faked death specifically engineered to frame Idris has gained massive traction. The media and the oversight committees are finally starting to connect the dots." She looked at Idris, a small nod of validation in his direction. "The information we released is cutting through the noise. For the first time, Lumivox is under a microscope that they can't just shatter. Bribing the local heavyweights isn't going to cut it this time, the scale of the fraud is too public, and the scent of blood is in the water. They're finally losing their grip on the narrative." Her expression then shifted, turning grave as she looked around the room. "But we have to be realistic. There are a lot of powerful people who stand to lose everything if this goes to trial, and they aren't going to let that happen quietly. We are fighting a giant, and a giant doesn't go down without swinging." She pulled a set of documents from her bag, her hands steady despite her exhaustion. "A few companies with vested interests in seeing Lumivox fall have stepped up. They've decided to sponsor a private cruise for all of you, a mobile safe haven that will serve as a high-end witness protection program. The condition is that you all agree to testify. They want a united front, with Idris as the star witness." She paused, her gaze darkening. "We tried to reach out to some of the Sector 7 people to bolster the case, but every single one of them refused to come forward. Like the three in the cells, they are paralyzed by fear. It's just us, which means your safety is now the state's highest priority and their biggest vulnerability." The group let out a heavy, synchronized sigh that seemed to vibrate in the small room. "So, back to the prison life?" Idris asked, his voice laced with a weary, ironic bitterness. After years of running through shadows, the idea of being confined again even for his own protection felt like a cruel punchline. "Well, a luxury one with every facility you can imagine," Belinda countered, taking the documents from her mother. She flipped through the glossy pages of the vessel's layout, but her hands were trembling. "Private decks, high-end suites... it's a floating palace." "It's a floating target," Peter interrupted, his voice cutting through the forced optimism. He walked over and tapped a finger on the ship's schematics. "Look at this. We'll be in international waters. That means no local police, no immediate backup, and only one way on or off. If Lumivox decides that the cost of an 'accident' at sea is cheaper than a multi-billion dollar settlement, we're sitting ducks in the middle of a blue void." The reality of his words chilled the air. They weren't being rewarded; they were being concentrated into a single coordinate on a map. "Peter's right," William added, his voice hollow as he looked up from his folded hands. "These other companies might be paying for the ship to keep us safe, but they're also herding us into a corner. We're the only evidence left. By putting us all on one boat, they've made it so Lumivox only has to succeed once to make this entire trial vanish. We aren't just witnesses anymore; we're the only targets left on the board." The weight of their vulnerability settled over them. The ship was a fortress, yes, but it was a fortress that could easily become a coffin if the enemy decided to sink the evidence rather than face it.

The following days were a frantic blur of motion as the group prepared for their disappearance. Each of them, driven by a sense of justice or the simple need for survival, had volunteered for the witness protection program. They were the only ones willing to put their names on the record to dismantle Lumivox, all except for Frank. The void Frank left behind was deafening. Callie had called him immediately after the meeting at Mrs. Potts', her heart hammering against her ribs, but Frank's refusal had been terrifyingly bland. There was no anger in his voice, only a hollow finality that was far more chilling. He told her he couldn't do it, he wouldn't be trapped on a boat while his family was out in the world, exposed. After that first call, the silence became absolute. Every subsequent attempt from the group went straight to voicemail, the digital tether cut as if he had never existed. On the final night, just hours before the convoy was set to move, Callie paced the porch of the safe house, the cold air biting at her skin. She tried his number one last time, her breath hitching as the phone rang and rang. When he finally picked up, her voice cracked as she pleaded with him to join them, to choose the safety of the ocean over the paranoia of the streets. But Frank was unreachable in every sense of the word. He wasn't just refusing a cruise; he was retreating into a fortress of his own making, and Callie realized with a sinking dread that no amount of logic could pull him out of the psychological storm Zach had unleashed.

The day to board the Azure Guardian arrived with a sky the color of lead, the heavy mist reflecting the somber reality on the pier. For months, Frank had been their steady hand, the silent leader who had built their defenses and stood between them and the abyss. Leaving without him didn't just feel like a loss; it felt like a betrayal of the unit he had worked so hard to forge. The dock was crowded with more than just security. Idris's parents and William's family had arrived to see them off, their presence a poignant reminder of the stakes. The air was filled with hushed whispers and tight, lingering embraces as parents clung to children they had almost lost to the shadows. Idris stood near the gangway, his eyes scanning the road one last time, hoping for a familiar car that he knew wasn't coming. He turned to Callie, his expression pained. He didn't need to speak; the question was etched into the lines of his face. "He's... he's attending a conference," Callie said. She forced the words out, but her voice lacked total conviction. It was the hollow excuse Frank had given her, a thin, professional mask to hide the fact that he was currently paralyzed by the psychological poison Zach had injected into his life. Idris looked at the others. Peter and William were staring at the ground, their usual bravado replaced by a hollow silence. Without Frank, they felt like a unit that had lost its captain right before the final battle. Mrs. Potts stepped into the center of the circle, her presence pulling them back from the edge of their despondency. She looked at each of them, her eyes mirroring their exhaustion but hardened by a fierce necessity. "Listen to me," she said, her voice a low, steady anchor that demanded their focus. "From the moment you step onto that vessel, your only job is to stay alive and stay ready. You are the testimony. You are the living proof of everything they tried to bury." She placed a firm hand on Idris's shoulder and looked at the group. "This ship is more than a cruise; it is a fortress. Do not take the luxury for granted. Use this time to sharpen your minds and rest your bodies. Lumivox is a giant, and they will be looking for a crack in your resolve. Do not give them one." She turned to the parents standing nearby, then back to the group. "Your families are my responsibility now. They will be safe under my roof, shielded by every resource I have. You focus on the courtroom; I will focus on the home front. No one gets to you, and no one gets to them. Now, board that ship and don't look back until the verdict is read." The parting with their families was brief and heavy, final kisses on cheeks and promises whispered against the wind. As the group began to ascend the gangway, the metal clanking under their boots felt like a funeral march for the life they used to know. They were moving toward a gilded safety, but the silence where Frank should have been followed them like a shadow into the deep.

The ship's engines thrummed with a deep, rhythmic pulse as the Azure Guardian cut through the dark Atlantic waters. Far behind them, the lights of the coastline had faded into nothingness, leaving the group alone in the vast, silent cradle of the ocean. They stood together on the stern deck, the salt spray misting their faces. The aggressive luxury of the ship, the plush carpets, the gourmet galleys, the high-end security felt surreal and distant, a gilded wrapper on a dangerous reality. Without Frank there to anchor them, the silence between them was heavy. They stood as a fractured mosaic: some shielding secrets that had yet to surface, others clinging to a fragile hope for the unexpected. A few felt the hollow ache of their missing guide, while the rest were still reeling from the raw sting of guilt, betrayal, and the ghosts of the past. "We're a long way from Mrs. Potts' living room," Idris began, his voice barely a whisper against the wind, yet it commanded the deck. "Feels more like a floating cage than a palace," Peter muttered, leaning against the cold steel. "No Frank, no backup. Just us and a thousand miles of water." "That's exactly what they want us to think," Idris countered, his eyes catching the faint moonlight. "They want us to feel small. They want us to feel like we're just 'collateral' or 'liabilities' waiting for a cleaner to find us. But they've made a mistake." William looked up, his face still etched with the weariness of his uncle's betrayal. "What mistake, Idris? We're the only ones left." "The mistake of putting us together," Idris said, his voice rising, growing more certain. "Lumivox thinks they're a giant because they have billions of dollars and a 'ghost' empire. But giants are clumsy. They're slow. And they are terrified of the truth we're carrying in our heads." He looked at Callie, Belinda, and Amanda, his gaze lingering on each of them until they stood a little taller. "Frank isn't here to lead us, but he's the one who taught us how to fight," Idris continued. "We aren't the victims anymore. We are the predators now. We've survived the warehouses, the kidnappings, and the lies. If Lumivox wants a war, they've finally met the only people who can actually win it." He stepped into the center of the circle, extending his hand. "We are the alphas of this story. We have one another, and as long as we stand on this deck together, that giant is already falling. We bring them down for Jordan, for the village, and for everyone they thought they could erase." One by one, they placed their hands over his. The dread that had suffocated them for days finally began to break, replaced by a cold, sharp resolve. "To the trial," Callie said, her voice finally finding its conviction. "To the trial," they echoed in unison. As the ship surged forward into the darkness, they weren't just witnesses anymore. They were a pack, and for the first time in years, the "ghosts" weren't the ones hunting.

Meanwhile, miles away in the shadows of the mainland, a sleek black sedan pulled up silently near the curb of a deserted street. The passenger window rolled down to reveal a balding man wearing thick glasses, his expression neutral and professional as he checked his watch. "Hey Frank," the man said, his voice a low, gravelly rasp. "Ready to rock and roll?" Frank stood in the shadows, his silhouette slumped with a bone-deep exhaustion. He looked like a man who had carried the weight of the world until his shoulders finally snapped. He didn't look back at the ocean or the life he was leaving behind. Instead, he forced a tired, ghost of a smile as he met the eyes of the man in the car. To the group, Frank was a leader seeking a hiding place at a "conference." But as the man in the sedan gave a subtle, respectful nod, it was clear that to this stranger, Frank was an asset finally being extracted. "Yes," Frank replied, his voice barely a whisper, heavy with a fatigue that seemed to reach his very soul. "Certainly, I am." He stepped into the car, the door closing with a heavy, pressurized thud. As the sedan pulled away and merged into the lightless highway, Frank leaned his head against the cold glass and closed his eyes. His phone remained dark in his pocket, ignoring the frantic pings from the ship. He wasn't heading to a witness box or a sanctuary; he was being moved like a piece on a different board entirely.
